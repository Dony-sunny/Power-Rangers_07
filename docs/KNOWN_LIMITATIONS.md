# Prototype boundaries

This is a local hackathon prototype. Shared quotes, integer monetary allocations, acceptance records, resource jobs, bookings, receipts and audits are persisted application behavior. Transport supply, tariffs, payment state, provider responses and positions remain simulated. No live dispatch, legal digital signature, official tax invoice, government submission, production payment settlement or credential-tested IBM integration is claimed.

The three main workspaces group the original fifteen roles. Demo switching is not production authentication. Signed identities retain their stored role; managed onboarding, SSO and policy administration need a pilot deployment. Tenant scoping remains authoritative on the server.

Text/document/OCR/spreadsheet/voice intake produces fields for human review. Broad Malayalam OCR and arbitrary real document accuracy have not been measured. Imported cargo needs a reviewed load profile before the new shared quotation flow. Legacy recurring service and planning tools retain their original scope; they are not evidence of live partner commitments.

Shared milestones are local execution records with simulated positions. Common voyage advancement remains ordered. Mid-voyage rescue, custody transfer and automatic load amendments are outside the supported recovery flow. A local receiver code is required only when a challenge was requested; milestone-only receipts must not be described as identity-verified.

Terminal appointments and crew assignments are coordination records. They do not establish employee qualification, labor availability or legal contracts. Provider jobs represent local simulated accepted commitments, not confirmations from external transport companies.

Public IWAI corridor information and Open-Meteo weather are [read-only reference context](DATA_PROVENANCE.md). Neither establishes present navigability or terminal availability. Weather can be unavailable and is never fabricated to conceal an outage.

SQLite is the verified prototype database. Production cross-provider concurrency, callback reconciliation, retention controls, PostgreSQL migrations and contractual compensation need separate validation. Passing the current tests supports the tested prototype behavior; it is not a guarantee that all future inputs or deployments are error-free.
